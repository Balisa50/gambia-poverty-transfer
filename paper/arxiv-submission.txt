TITLE
-----
Do prediction intervals for satellite-based poverty estimates survive a
national border? A pre-registered evaluation in West Africa

ABSTRACT  (plain text, 1,904 characters; arXiv limit 1,920)
================================================================

Satellite-based models of household wealth are validated on clusters from one
set of countries, then applied to countries with no survey, so accuracy is
measured under conditions unlike deployment. We ask whether their reported
uncertainty stays valid across a national border, using cluster-level wealth
index values from twelve West African Demographic and Health Surveys (6,706
clusters, 155,796 households) and open geospatial covariates. The Gambia was
withheld from all model development and evaluated once against a
pre-registered threshold.

Four hypotheses were fixed before data access and none was supported. Point
accuracy fell about three percent across a border, not the predicted third
(R^2 = 0.746 in-country under spatial blocking, 0.723 leave-one-country-out,
0.673 on the target). Mean conformal coverage also transferred: a nominal 90%
interval covered 88.7% of held-out foreign clusters and 89.3% of Gambian
clusters, against a registered prediction of substantially less. Correlation
alignment degraded accuracy. The registered target test returned a null at 53%
power.

Two observations are carried as leads. Coverage varied across held-out
countries by 3.7 times what sampling noise permits (0.821 to 0.979 at nominal
90%), so the average hides per-country spread. On the target, constant-width
split conformal undercovered at nominal 50% and 80% by over four standard
errors while holding at 90% and 95%; adaptive CQR did not.

Coverage holding here is a contrast, not a foregone conclusion: concurrent
work measuring conformal coverage on an African satellite product under
spatial shift reports it falling to 65.3% against a 90% target. Transfer of
wealth models is better behaved than distribution-shift caution implies, among
neighbouring countries sharing a survey instrument, and the residual risk lies
in interval shape and per-country spread rather than average coverage.
