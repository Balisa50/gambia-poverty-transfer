TITLE
-----
Do prediction intervals for satellite-based poverty estimates survive a
national border? A pre-registered evaluation in West Africa

ABSTRACT  (plain text, 1,917 characters; arXiv limit 1,920)
================================================================

Satellite-based models of household wealth are validated on survey clusters
from one set of countries, then applied to countries with no survey at all,
so accuracy is measured under conditions that do not match deployment. We
ask whether the uncertainty such models report stays valid across a national
border, using cluster-level wealth index values from twelve West African
Demographic and Health Surveys (6,706 clusters, 155,796 households) and open
geospatial covariates. The Gambia was withheld from all model development
and evaluated once against a pre-registered threshold.

Four hypotheses were fixed before data access and none was supported. Point
accuracy fell by about three percent across a border rather than the
predicted third (R^2 = 0.746 in-country under spatial blocking, 0.723 under
leave-one-country-out, 0.673 on the target). Mean conformal coverage also
transferred: a nominal 90% interval covered 88.7% of held-out foreign
clusters and 89.3% of Gambian clusters, against a registered prediction of
substantially less than 90%. Correlation alignment degraded accuracy rather
than improving it. The registered target test returned a null at 53% power.

Two observations are carried as leads. Coverage varied across held-out
countries by 3.7 times what sampling noise permits (0.821 to 0.979 at
nominal 90%), so the average hides per-country spread. On the target,
constant-width split conformal undercovered at nominal 50% and 80% by over
four standard errors while holding at 90% and 95%; adaptive conformalized
quantile regression did not.

Concurrent work reports one coverage figure for per-country fits; whether
coverage survives transfer, resolved per country and across nominal levels,
had not been reported. Cross-border transfer is better behaved than
distribution-shift caution implies, and the residual risk lies in interval
shape and per-country spread rather than average coverage.
